Guide

Check your LLM-as-a-judge in 5 steps. Each step shows the command, what you see, and how you know it worked.

Install

You need eval results already graded by an LLM-as-a-judge: from promptfoo, DeepEval, Inspect AI, MLflow, or your own CSV or JSONL file. In your project folder, run:

uvx judgekeeper

No uv? Run pip install judgekeeper, then judgekeeper. Needs Python 3.11 or newer, on macOS, Linux or Windows.

Open source under the MIT license. It's on PyPI, and the changelog lists what changed.

Step 1 · Find

Run judgekeeper in your project

It finds your eval results and the judge that graded them. It checks, with no AI call, that your judge gave every answer a clear Pass or Fail, then opens the marking page.

judgekeeper
example project
$ judgekeeper
judgekeeper 1.0.0b2

Step 1 of 5: Find

Eval tool:   promptfoo
Answers:     40 answers, each graded by your judge
             (read from results.json)
Judge:       openai:gpt-4.1-mini
Judge rule:  "The answer solves the customer's problem and is polite."

Your judge's grades: 23 Pass, 17 Fail.
Checked: your judge gave every answer a clear Pass or Fail.

Step 2 of 5: Mark

The marking page is open in your browser. It shows the answers
one at a time. Mark each one Pass or Fail, the way you want your
judge to decide. Your judge's grade stays hidden.

Next: mark until "See your result" appears, then click it.

Done when the marking page is open in your browser.

Didn't work?

No saved results yet? Run your eval once, then judgekeeper again.

Nothing found? Check you are in your project folder, or point it at the file: judgekeeper path/to/results.json.

Step 2 · Mark

Mark answers Pass or Fail

Mark each answer the way you want your judge to decide. Your judge's grade stays hidden. Use the buttons or the arrow keys. Every mark is saved, so you can close the page and come back.

Done when See your result appears at the top. Click it.

Step 3 · Measure

Read your result

The result page tells you if your judge is good enough to use, needs work, or if it is too early to tell. It shows where you and your judge disagreed. It is also saved as .judgekeeper/report.html.

Done when you see your result. Too early to tell? Mark more answers with judgekeeper mark.

Step 4 · Fix

Write a better rule and test it

Judge needs work? Run judgekeeper fix. It saves the clues and every mistake in one file you can hand to a coding agent.

On your result page, say why on the answers you and your judge disagree on; fix puts your reasons next to its mistakes.

judgekeeper fix
example project
$ judgekeeper fix
judgekeeper 1.0.0b2

Step 4 of 5: Fix

Clues:
  Half of its 10 mistakes are answers it passed but you failed,
  and half are answers it failed but you passed.

The clues and all 10 mistakes are saved in
.judgekeeper/fix/mistakes.md. You can hand this file to a coding
agent.

Your judge's rule is in promptfooconfig.yaml, line 14:
  "The answer solves the customer's problem and is polite."

12 more of your marks are kept aside to test your new rule
fairly.

Next: write your new rule in .judgekeeper/fix/rule.txt, then run
`judgekeeper fix` again to test it.

Write your new rule in .judgekeeper/fix/rule.txt and run judgekeeper fix again. It tests the rule on marks kept aside, and asks before it calls your judge. For promptfoo, it runs the version your results were made with, and the same question asks before it downloads that version with npx.

example project
$ judgekeeper fix
judgekeeper 1.0.0b2

Step 4 of 5: Fix (testing your new rule)

New rule:  "The answer fully solves the customer's problem, gives a c…"
           (from .judgekeeper/fix/rule.txt)

To test it, judgekeeper asks your judge about the 12 answers
kept aside, once with the old rule and once with the new one.
That is 24 AI calls, paid with your own key.
Go ahead? [y/N] y

Tested on the 12 marks kept aside:
  Old rule agreed with you on 6. New rule agreed with you on 12.
  It fixed 6 and broke 0.

Result: your new rule is better. A difference this large is very
unlikely to be a coincidence.

Next: if you like it, copy the new rule into
promptfooconfig.yaml, line 14. Then run your eval and
`judgekeeper check`.

What the result means

ResultWhat to do
BetterUse the new rule.
Safe to tryNot clearly better yet, and very unlikely to be more than 10 points worse. You can try it.
Can't tell yetMark a few more answers and test again.
Looks worse, or WorseKeep the old rule.

Done when you have a result. To use the new rule, copy it into your eval config, run your eval, then judgekeeper check.

Step 5 · Re-check

Re-check after you change your judge

Changed the model, the rule or your eval tool's version? Run your eval, then:

judgekeeper check

It tells you if your judge still agrees with you, on the same marks. It asks before any AI call.

example project
$ judgekeeper check
judgekeeper 1.0.0b2

Step 5 of 5: Re-check

Your judge changed: model openai:gpt-4.1-mini -> openai:gpt-4.1-nano.

To re-check, judgekeeper asks your new judge about your 30
marked answers, twice each. That is 60 AI calls, paid with your
own key. Asking twice shows how much the judge varies by itself.
Go ahead? [y/N] y

Re-checked on all 30 of your marks:

  You passed 15 answers. Your old judge agreed with you on 7,
  your new judge on 1.
    Worse: 6 answers went from right to wrong, and none from wrong to right.
    That is about 40 points lower, and could be as much as 61.

  You failed 15 answers. Your old judge agreed with you on 7,
  your new judge on 7.
    Not sure: 1 went from right to wrong, and 1 from wrong to
    right. It could be up to 20 points worse.

Asked twice, your new judge changed its own grade on 3 of your
30 answers. Differences that small can come from that alone.

These marks have helped pick 1 change.

Next: go back to the old judge, or change your judge and run
`judgekeeper check` again.

Moved to a different eval tool? Start fresh with judgekeeper --new: judgekeeper 1.0 can't compare judges across two tools.

Keys and costs

Steps 1 to 3 make no AI calls and need no key. Testing a new rule, and some re-checks, call your judge through your own eval tool and its key. judgekeeper says how many calls first and waits for your yes. It never prints or saves a key.

Your own format

No eval tool? Save one row per answer in a CSV or JSONL file:

ColumnWhat goes in it
inputThe question or prompt.
outputThe answer that was graded.
verdictYour judge's grade: pass or fail (true/false, yes/no and 1/0 work too).

Judge gives a score? Say where Pass starts: judgekeeper --pass-if 'score>=0.5'.

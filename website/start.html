Install judgekeeper
in your project.

Works with promptfoo, DeepEval, Inspect AI, MLflow or your own file.

your project folder

With pip, in your project’s virtual environment

pip install judgekeeper
judgekeeper

With uv

uvx judgekeeper

Works on macOS, Linux and Windows, with Python 3.11 to 3.14.

Using a coding agent? Give it this page. It runs the steps and gives you the marking page link; you mark, then it explains the result.

The 5 steps.

  1. Find.
  2. Mark.
  3. Measure.
  4. Fix.
  5. Re-check.

  1. Find.

    Find your eval results

    Run judgekeeper in your project folder. It finds your eval results and opens the marking page.

    Nothing found?
    Run your eval first so it saves its results, or point judgekeeper to your results file.
    your project folder
    judgekeeper
    judgekeeper path/to/results.json

  2. Mark.

    Mark your answers Pass or Fail

    Mark each answer Pass or Fail, the way you want your LLM-as-a-judge to decide. You won’t see your LLM-as-a-judge’s grade, so it can’t sway you. Every mark is saved, so you can stop at any time and come back later.

    When See the numbers so far or See your result appears at the top, click it.

    Already have Pass/Fail labels?
    Run judgekeeper --labels your-labels.csv, or judgekeeper --labels if they are in your results. Then mark more if needed.
    marking page

    Tap to enlarge

  3. Measure.

    See how often your LLM-as-a-judge agrees with you

    The result page tells you one of three things: your LLM-as-a-judge is good enough to use, it needs work, or it’s too early to tell. Below that, it lists the answers where you and your LLM-as-a-judge disagreed.

    Result says it’s too early to tell?
    Your LLM-as-a-judge needs more of your marks to be measured. Mark more answers, then look again.
    judgekeeper mark
    result page

    Tap to enlarge

  4. Fix.

    Write a better rule and test it

    Result says your LLM-as-a-judge needs work?
    It saves the answers your LLM-as-a-judge got wrong in .judgekeeper/fix/mistakes.md, apart from the marks kept aside to test your rule. Read it to write a better rule yourself, or hand it to a coding agent to help you write one.

    Change the rule in your eval tool. Have it grade the answers in .judgekeeper/fix/marked-answers.csv again, then save the results.

    judgekeeper tests the new rule on the marks kept aside and tells you if it’s better, safe to try, worse, or if it can’t tell yet. How many marks you need depends on how big the change is: see the reference.

    your project folder
    judgekeeper fix
    judgekeeper fix new-results.json

    judgekeeper prints how to grade the answers again with your eval tool. It only reads the new results.

  5. Re-check.

    Re-check after you change your LLM-as-a-judge

    Changed your LLM-as-a-judge’s model or rule?
    Grade your marked answers again (judgekeeper fix writes them to .judgekeeper/fix/marked-answers.csv).

    judgekeeper compares the new grades with your marks and tells you if your LLM-as-a-judge still agrees with you. You don’t need to mark anything again.

    your project folder
    judgekeeper check new-results.json

Your own file or code

No eval tool? Save your results as a CSV, TSV or JSONL file with three columns: input (the question), output (the answer) and verdict (Pass or Fail from your LLM-as-a-judge). Other names work too: judgekeeper asks how to read them. Then run:

judgekeeper results.csv

Your LLM-as-a-judge runs in your own code? Add this after each grade:

import judgekeeper
judgekeeper.record(input=question, output=answer, verdict=grade)

It saves in .judgekeeper/records/ in your project folder. Run judgekeeper there.

Your LLM-as-a-judge gives a score instead of Pass or Fail? The full reference shows how to set where Pass starts, and every other option.

No API key needed

In your project, judgekeeper reads your results files and writes only in .judgekeeper/, plus judgekeeper.toml after you say yes. It makes no AI call and needs no API key. When you grade answers again, your own eval tool makes the calls, with your own key.

Handy commands

$ judgekeeper --stop         # close the marking page
$ judgekeeper --new          # start over (old marks are kept)
$ judgekeeper --no-browser   # get the link, no browser
$ judgekeeper --help         # see every option

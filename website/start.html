Start in
one command.

You need eval results already graded by an LLM-as-a-judge: from promptfoo, DeepEval, Inspect AI, MLflow, or your own CSV or JSONL file.

In your project folder, run

uvx judgekeeper

No uv? Run pip install judgekeeper, then judgekeeper. Needs Python 3.11 or newer, on macOS, Linux or Windows.

The 5 steps.

  1. 1

    Find your results

    Run judgekeeper in your project. It finds your eval results and the judge that graded them. It checks, with no AI call, that your judge gave every answer a clear Pass or Fail, then opens the marking page.

    judgekeeper

    Done when the marking page is open in your browser.

    Didn't work?

    No saved results yet? Run your eval once, then judgekeeper again.

    Nothing found? Check you are in your project folder, or point it at the file: judgekeeper path/to/results.json.

  2. 2

    Mark answers Pass or Fail

    Mark each answer the way you want your judge to decide. Your judge's grade stays hidden. Use the buttons or the arrow keys. Every mark is saved, so you can close the page and come back.

    Done when See your result appears at the top. Click it.

  3. 3

    Read your result

    The result page tells you if your judge is good enough to use, needs work, or if it is too early to tell. It shows where you and your judge disagreed. It is also saved as .judgekeeper/report.html.

    Done when you see your result. Too early to tell? Mark more answers with judgekeeper mark.

    How the numbers are worked out

  4. 4

    Write a better rule and test it

    Judge needs work? Run judgekeeper fix. It saves the clues and every mistake in one file you can hand to a coding agent. On your result page, say why on the answers you and your judge disagree on; fix puts your reasons next to its mistakes.

    judgekeeper fix

    Write your new rule in .judgekeeper/fix/rule.txt and run judgekeeper fix again. It tests the rule on marks kept aside, and asks before it calls your judge.

    ResultWhat to do
    BetterUse the new rule.
    Safe to tryNot clearly better yet, and very unlikely to be more than 10 points worse. You can try it.
    Can't tell yetMark a few more answers and test again. If it says it leans toward worse, keep the old rule.
    Looks worse, or WorseKeep the old rule.

    Done when you have a result. To use the new rule, copy it into your eval config, run your eval, then judgekeeper check.

  5. 5

    Re-check after you change your judge

    Changed the model, the rule or your eval tool's version? Run your eval, then:

    judgekeeper check

    It tells you if your judge still agrees with you, on the same marks. It asks before any AI call. The result is Worse, No drop bigger than 20 points, or Not sure.

    Moved to a different eval tool? Start fresh with judgekeeper --new: judgekeeper 1.0 can't compare judges across two tools.

Keys and costs

Steps 1 to 3 make no AI calls and need no key. Testing a new rule, and some re-checks, call your judge through your own eval tool and its key. judgekeeper says how many calls first and waits for your yes. It never prints or saves a key.

Your own format

No eval tool? Save one row per answer in a CSV or JSONL file with three columns: input (the question), output (the answer that was graded) and verdict (pass or fail; true/false, yes/no and 1/0 work too).

Judge gives a score? Say where Pass starts: judgekeeper --pass-if 'score>=0.5'.

When your judge runs in your own code and saves nothing judgekeeper reads, add one line right after it gives each grade, in your eval code:

import judgekeeper
judgekeeper.record(input=question, output=answer, score=0.82, pass_mark=0.5,
                   reason=reason, judge="claude-opus-5", rule=criteria, name="Safe wording")

Then run your eval once and judgekeeper: it finds the records. Install judgekeeper inside your project's environment (pip install judgekeeper) for the import.

Handy commands

judgekeeper --stopClose the marking page
judgekeeper --newStart over (your old marks are kept)
judgekeeper --no-browserGet the link instead of opening a browser
judgekeeper --helpSee every option
